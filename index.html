FEATURED CASE STUDY · CONCEPTUAL RECREATION

Emergency Mustering — from incident to operational overview

Consider a fire or other critical incident affecting a building, floor or workplace area. People present may include employees and visitors. Administrators need a fast way to understand who has been mustered, who is still missing or unaccounted for, and who may need assistance.

01 · Incident Context

An active emergency is selected from a left-side emergency list, opening the corresponding operational workspace.

02 · Situational View

The workspace brings together summary widgets, grid-based personnel information and map-oriented context.

03 · Actionable Status

Administrators can quickly review mustered, missing/unaccounted and assistance-required states.

Data & operational visibility

Relevant personnel information is obtained from the available attendance summary and presented in the context of the emergency. This gives administrators an overview of mustering progress and visibility into the time taken during the response process.

Architecture principle

The dashboard was not approached as a one-off screen. UI elements were structured for reuse and configurability, helping support varying requirements across multiple enterprise clients while keeping the implementation maintainable.

Reusable Components→Configuration→Data Integration→Multi-client Use

My contribution

Requirement clarification, UI architecture, component design and development, integration discussions, stakeholder demonstrations, feedback incorporation, QA collaboration, troubleshooting and production support.

CASE STUDY · IDENTITY & ACCESS MANAGEMENT

Configurable identity lifecycle & access management

I worked on selected areas of a large enterprise Identity & Access Management application, taking ownership of UI changes and enhancements as new business requirements were introduced. The platform supported identity onboarding, badge and asset operations, physical/location access, system access, approvals and auditability.

01 · Self-Service Requests

A common portal supported different request types including non-employee onboarding, bulk onboarding, badge assignment, badge activation/deactivation, location access and system/software access.

02 · Configurable Experience

Request fields and request flows were designed to be configurable so the same platform could support changing requirements and different client needs without treating every request as a separate UI.

03 · Operational Management

Dedicated screens supported management of identities, locations/access and card/badge assets, together with audit history for actions performed in the system.

Configurable request & approval flow

A request could move through different approval responsibilities depending on its purpose—for example, badge-related approval by a badge administrator or location-access approval by an area administrator. The UI needed to make the current request, required action and history understandable while supporting configurable workflows.

Request Type→Configurable Fields→Approval Flow→Provision / Manage→Audit History

Badge design & lifecycle

The application also included badge-management capabilities covering assignment and activation/deactivation. I worked with a badge-designing screen where SVG-based capabilities were used to support the visual badge configuration experience.

License Manager

I also worked on a License Manager area used to maintain client organization agreement/licensing information. It included duration-based configuration and reminder options to help track relevant agreement timelines.

UI architecture focus

Because the application supported multiple clients and a broad set of identity and access workflows, the front-end approach emphasized reusable components, configurable fields and configurable request flows. This reduced the need for one-off implementations and made requirement changes easier to accommodate.
